# Related-Party and Conflict Policy

This document records intended boundaries for related-party work, contributor
support, personal research, official support, and potential conflicts of
interest involving PaperProof Labs. It is project documentation, not legal
advice.

## Purpose

Early-stage protocol projects may involve overlapping roles. The same person
or group may contribute personally, maintain official project infrastructure,
write research, operate official channels, publish content, build software,
attend events, or receive support for ecosystem work.

This policy is intended to allow useful PaperProof ecosystem work while
avoiding false independence, undisclosed self-dealing, misleading official
endorsement, or token-value implications.

## Relationship to Protocol and Labs

PaperProof Protocol refers to the open protocol layer and official deployed
protocol instances. PaperProof Labs refers to the originating team and
maintainer of the official interface, SDKs, reference indexer, documentation,
and brand identity.

Individual contributors, maintainers, operators, researchers, developers, or
community members may act in personal, contributor, researcher, contractor,
operator, official, or mixed capacities. The relevant capacity should be made
clear where it matters.

## Permitted Related-Party Work

PaperProof Labs may support, reference, fund, reimburse, host, publish,
promote, or collaborate with work by contributors, maintainers, operators,
researchers, developers, or community members, including where those persons
also have official PaperProof Labs responsibilities.

Examples may include:

- research papers, yellow papers, whitepapers, technical notes, or academic
  submissions;
- protocol design, SDK, indexer, app, documentation, or tooling work;
- blog posts, tutorials, translations, diagrams, slides, videos, or demos;
- event attendance, hackathon work, conference participation, or community
  presentations;
- bug reports, security review, testing, deployment support, or operations;
- ecosystem grants, contributor compensation, reimbursements, or research
  support.

Such support should be connected to PaperProof Protocol, PaperProof Labs,
ecosystem development, documentation, research, security, product quality,
community education, or operational needs.

## Disclosure

Where a related-party relationship could reasonably matter to readers, users,
reviewers, funders, contributors, or ecosystem participants, PaperProof Labs
should disclose the relationship in plain language.

Suggested wording:

```text
The author/contributor also has a role with PaperProof Labs. This work is
published in a personal or contributor capacity and may be supported,
referenced, hosted, or promoted by PaperProof Labs where relevant to the
PaperProof ecosystem.
```

Another suggested wording:

```text
PaperProof Labs support for this work is related to protocol research,
documentation, ecosystem development, or project operations. Such support does
not imply any token value, investment return, market support, or guaranteed
reward.
```

## No False Independence

PaperProof Labs should not present a related party as an independent external
third party where the relationship would be material.

For example, official communications should avoid implying that a contributor,
researcher, reviewer, or partner is fully independent if that person also
controls, maintains, operates, or receives material support from PaperProof
Labs in the relevant context.

It is acceptable to collaborate with related parties. It is not acceptable to
use related-party collaboration to create misleading third-party validation,
fake independence, undisclosed endorsement, or artificial community support.

## Support and Compensation Records

Where PaperProof Labs provides material support to a related party, it should
preserve reasonable records, such as:

- supported person or role category;
- support type;
- amount, token amount, reimbursement amount, or resource provided;
- purpose and ecosystem rationale;
- date and approval path;
- transaction digest, invoice, receipt, or supporting record where applicable;
- whether the support is contributor compensation, research support,
  reimbursement, grant, bounty, operations support, event support, or another
  category;
- any public disclosure, if appropriate.

Records help distinguish legitimate ecosystem support from unexplained
official-to-personal transfers or undisclosed self-dealing.

## Token and Market Boundaries

Related-party support must not be described as:

- an investment return;
- token-price support;
- market-making support;
- liquidity support;
- compensation for buying, holding, trading, or promoting PPRF;
- evidence that PPRF has or will have market value;
- a guarantee of future grants, rewards, allocations, or ecosystem support.

PaperProof Labs support for related-party work should be framed as ecosystem
development, research support, contributor compensation, reimbursement,
operations support, documentation support, or similar non-investment purposes.

## Official Resource Use

Official PaperProof Labs resources may be used for related-party work when the
work reasonably supports PaperProof Protocol or the PaperProof ecosystem.
Examples may include official hosting, links, publication, citation, demo
support, design support, technical review, infrastructure, or limited funding.

Such use should not create confusion about whether the work is personal,
contributor-authored, officially authored, independently reviewed, or formally
endorsed.

## Review and Recusal

Where practical, material related-party support should be reviewed using a
clear approval path. If PaperProof Labs later has multiple operators, reviewers,
committee members, or governance participants, persons with a direct conflict
should consider disclosure, recusal, or independent review where appropriate.

In very early stages, where independent review is not practical, PaperProof
Labs should rely on clear records, public disclosure where appropriate, and
reasonable consistency with published policy.

## No Guarantee

This policy does not guarantee that any contributor, maintainer, operator,
researcher, developer, community member, or related party will receive support,
funding, reimbursement, promotion, publication, official status, or future
opportunities.

PaperProof Labs may approve, decline, modify, delay, or discontinue
related-party support where it considers appropriate for legal, operational,
financial, security, ecosystem, reputation, or policy reasons.
