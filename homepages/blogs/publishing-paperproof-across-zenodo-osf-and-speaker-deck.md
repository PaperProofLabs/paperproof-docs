# Publishing PaperProof Across Zenodo, OSF, and Speaker Deck

Author: PaperProof Labs  
Category: Ecosystem and Discoverability  
Status: Official Blog Draft  
Suggested Artifact Type: `blog_post`  

PaperProof is built as protocol infrastructure for versioned, verifiable
artifacts on Sui and Walrus. But if people cannot easily discover the project's
papers and slides through the wider internet, then an important part of the
protocol's public reach remains underdeveloped.

That is why we have started publishing core PaperProof research and
presentation materials across established public knowledge platforms in
addition to publishing through the protocol itself.

This is not a shift away from PaperProof-native publishing. It is a recognition
that durable protocol artifacts and durable public discoverability should
reinforce each other.

## Why this matters

For a project like PaperProof, publishing papers and slides is not only about
documentation. It is about public positioning.

PaperProof sits at the intersection of:

- artifact publishing;
- verifiable content infrastructure;
- Sui and Walrus ecosystem tooling;
- developer-facing protocol design;
- agent-readable public knowledge.

That combination is unusual. It means many people who may care about the
project are not all looking in the same place. Some discover ideas through
GitHub. Some through a project website. Some through preprint archives. Some
through repository platforms with DOI-backed public records. Some through slide
sharing sites. Some through search engines indexing all of the above.

By publishing across Zenodo, OSF MetaArXiv, and Speaker Deck, PaperProof is
not depending on a single discovery path.

## Why these three platforms were chosen

Each platform serves a different public function.

### Zenodo

Zenodo is useful because it creates durable public records for the papers and
slides in a format that is friendly to long-term discovery, citation, and
archival reference. For a protocol project, that matters because white papers,
yellow papers, academic papers, and technical slides are not disposable
marketing content. They are part of the project's durable public knowledge
surface.

### OSF MetaArXiv

OSF MetaArXiv helps place the papers into a research-oriented preprint context.
That does not make PaperProof an academic-only project, but it does make the
project easier to encounter through research and scholarly infrastructure
channels. This is especially relevant for a protocol whose scope includes
preprints, technical reports, datasets, software releases, and long-lived
knowledge artifacts.

### Speaker Deck

Speaker Deck serves a different role. It is a lightweight public distribution
surface for the presentation itself. For many readers, a slide deck is the
fastest way to understand what PaperProof is, where it sits in the Sui and
Walrus stack, and why an artifact layer matters. Slides are not a substitute
for deeper papers, but they are often the fastest entry point into the system.

## Why publishing externally does not weaken protocol-native publishing

One might ask whether publishing to external platforms undermines the case for
PaperProof itself.

We think the opposite is true.

A protocol-native artifact system should not require the entire world to change
its reading habits before the protocol becomes useful. If PaperProof is serious
about becoming infrastructure, then its own flagship materials should be able
to exist both:

- as protocol-native artifacts; and
- as discoverable public records on widely used internet platforms.

This dual presence demonstrates an important principle: PaperProof is not
trying to trap content inside one website or one closed distribution path. It
is trying to give important digital work stronger identity, versioning,
verification, and interoperability, while still allowing that work to travel
through the broader web.

## Why this is strategically valuable for PaperProof

There are at least five strategic benefits.

### 1. It improves search visibility

High-quality public records on established domains make it easier for people to
encounter PaperProof through ordinary search behavior. That matters especially
for terms like `PaperProof`, `PaperProof Protocol`, `PaperProof Sui`, and
`PaperProof Walrus`.

### 2. It strengthens conceptual legitimacy

When a project has a white paper, a yellow paper, an academic-style paper, and
a public slide deck available through recognized public platforms, it becomes
easier for outsiders to see that the project is attempting to define a real
protocol category rather than only shipping a narrow app surface.

### 3. It reaches different audiences

The same material does not work equally well for every audience:

- a researcher may prefer a preprint page;
- a builder may prefer GitHub and docs;
- an ecosystem operator may prefer a technical slide deck;
- a broader public reader may prefer a downloadable archival record.

Publishing in multiple formats makes the project more legible across those
audiences.

### 4. It helps PaperProof occupy its own keyword space

Public releases across multiple high-trust platforms increase the number of
searchable pages associated with the PaperProof name. Over time, that helps the
project claim more of its own identity surface on the public internet.

### 5. It showcases the type of artifacts PaperProof is designed for

PaperProof is not only for blog posts. It is designed for preprints, technical
reports, datasets, software releases, and other durable digital work. By
publishing papers and slides in multiple public forms, the project is also
demonstrating the kind of artifact ecology it wants to support more broadly.

## The deeper protocol lesson

There is also a more important lesson here.

The web still lacks good infrastructure for treating important digital work as
first-class, versioned, verifiable, portable artifacts. Research papers,
slides, datasets, reports, and software releases are often scattered across
unconnected systems. Discovery happens in one place, storage in another,
discussion in another, and version tracking somewhere else entirely.

PaperProof exists because that fragmentation is still a real problem.

Publishing our own flagship materials across external knowledge platforms does
not solve that fragmentation by itself. But it helps demonstrate both the
problem and the opportunity:

- public distribution still matters;
- durable records still matter;
- versioned artifacts still matter;
- and a protocol layer that can unify these concerns still matters.

## Looking forward

This publication set should be seen as a beginning, not an endpoint.

The long-term goal is not only for PaperProof to have papers and slides visible
on the public internet. The larger goal is for PaperProof to become part of the
infrastructure through which many kinds of durable digital work are published,
verified, discovered, versioned, discussed, and reused.

In that sense, these releases do more than expand visibility. They help make
the case that PaperProof belongs in the broader public knowledge landscape, not
only inside its own protocol interface.
