PaperProof does not have one perfect one-to-one competitor in web3. It overlaps
with several project families, each of which matches only part of the
protocol's scope.

The cleanest way to understand the landscape is to see PaperProof as sitting at
the intersection of:

- onchain publishing;
- open social protocols;
- verifiable artifact infrastructure.

## The closest overlapping projects

The most relevant comparison set is:

- Paragraph and the earlier Mirror model;
- Lens Protocol;
- Farcaster;
- DeSo;
- Crossbell.

These projects matter because they overlap with publication, ownership, social
coordination, or protocol composability. But none of them makes the artifact
series and version lineage the center of the design in the same way.

## Comparison table

| Project | Core center | Where it overlaps with PaperProof | Where PaperProof differs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paragraph / Mirror | Web3 publishing, audience, subscription, creator distribution | Both care about onchain publication and creator ownership | Paragraph is closer to a publishing product; PaperProof is closer to an artifact protocol with typed versions, comments bindings, and verifiable content commitments |
| Lens | Open social graph and application primitives | Both allow multiple apps to build on shared protocol state | Lens centers profiles, follows, feeds, groups, and social rules; PaperProof centers artifact series, version history, and content identity |
| Farcaster | Decentralized social network protocol | Both are protocol layers rather than a single app | Farcaster optimizes identity, messages, channels, and clients; PaperProof optimizes long-lived knowledge artifacts and official artifact-bound discussion |
| DeSo | Social-native blockchain and creator economy | Both open content data to application layers | DeSo is broad social-chain infrastructure; PaperProof is narrower but deeper around verifiable artifact lifecycle |
| Crossbell | Ownership-oriented creator and social protocol | Both have protocol-native content ownership flavor | Crossbell leans toward creator/social identity and activity ownership; PaperProof leans toward formal artifacts, versioning, and governed knowledge records |

## The main distinction

The strongest web3 competitors are generally optimized around:

- creators and audiences;
- profiles and social graphs;
- posts and feeds;
- community distribution.

PaperProof is optimized around:

- the continuing work;
- exact versions;
- reproducible content references;
- official discussion and participation objects;
- agent-readable protocol state.

That makes it closer to a knowledge coordination layer than to a social-first
protocol.

## Why this matters strategically

If an ecosystem already has many feed-oriented protocols, another feed protocol
must fight for distribution and social habit. But a protocol that cleanly owns
the artifact identity and lifecycle layer can become complementary to many
frontends at once.

In that sense, PaperProof can be used by:

- official PaperProof surfaces;
- independent research portals;
- release dashboards;
- governance dashboards;
- wallets and explorers;
- agent and Copilot-style tools;
- even external publishing interfaces that want verifiable artifact backing.

## Competitive conclusion

In web3, PaperProof is best understood not as "another social protocol" but as
an artifact-centric coordination layer that can coexist with publishing and
social interfaces while solving a different underlying problem.
